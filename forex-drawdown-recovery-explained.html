Forex Drawdown Recovery Explained: Why Big Losses Are So Hard to Win Back

By Forex Profit Pathways · Updated 30 October 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer: Drawdown is the drop from your account's peak to a lower point. Recovery needed = drawdown / (1 - drawdown). A 50% loss needs a 100% gain to get back to even, which is why controlling losses matters more than chasing profits.

Beginners often think a 50% loss can be fixed with a 50% gain. It cannot. The math works against you, and understanding it is one of the strongest reasons to keep your risk small.

What is drawdown?

Drawdown is the percentage your account falls from a peak balance (or equity) to a later low. If your account grows to $10,000 and then falls to $8,000, your drawdown is 20%. The largest such fall in a period is called the maximum drawdown.

Why recovery needs a bigger percentage

After a loss, you have less money. The next gain is calculated on that smaller amount.

Example: $10,000 drops 20% to $8,000. To return to $10,000 you need $2,000 of profit. That is 2,000 / 8,000 = 25%, not 20%.

How risk per trade changes your drawdown

Imagine you lose 10 trades in a row. Every strategy has losing streaks.

  • Risking 1% per trade: account falls about 9.6% (1 - 0.9910). You need about 10.6% to recover.
  • Risking 5% per trade: account falls about 40%. You need about 66.7% to recover.
  • Risking 10% per trade: account falls about 65%. You need about 186% to recover.

The strategy is identical in each case. Only the risk per trade differs. Learn how to set it in our guide on how to calculate lot size.

Simple rules to limit drawdown

  1. Risk 1% or less per trade while you are learning.
  2. Set a daily loss limit, for example 2% to 3%. When you hit it, stop trading for the day.
  3. Set a weekly or monthly limit, and review your plan if you reach it.
  4. Reduce your lot size after a losing streak instead of increasing it.
  5. Avoid opening many trades on related pairs, since they can lose together.
  6. Plan reward as well as risk. See risk reward ratio explained.

The psychology of drawdown

Large losses push traders to take bigger risks to win the money back quickly. This usually makes things worse. If you feel the urge to "get it back", close the platform and review your trades in a trade journal.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good maximum drawdown?

It depends on the trader and strategy. Many risk-conscious traders try to keep drawdowns below 10% to 20%, because deeper drawdowns are hard to recover from.

Why does a 50% loss need a 100% gain?

After losing half your money you have half the capital. To get back to the starting amount you must double what remains.

What is the difference between balance drawdown and equity drawdown?

Balance drawdown only counts closed trades. Equity drawdown also counts floating profit and loss on open trades, so it shows the real dips.

How can I recover from a drawdown?

Do not increase risk. Keep your risk per trade small, follow your plan, and let normal trading rebuild the account. Review your journal to find what went wrong.
