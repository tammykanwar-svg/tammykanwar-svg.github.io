How to Keep a Forex Trading Journal: What to Record and How to Review It

By Forex Profit Pathways · Updated 30 October 2026 · 8 min read
Short answer: A forex trading journal is a record of every trade you take: the setup, entry, stop loss, result and your emotions. Reviewing it weekly shows what really works, what mistakes repeat, and whether your strategy has a positive edge.

Most traders remember their big wins and forget their small, repeated mistakes. A journal fixes that. It turns your memory into data, so you improve using facts instead of feelings.

Why a trading journal matters

  • It shows the truth about your results. Your win rate and average reward are often different from what you think.
  • It exposes repeated mistakes, such as moving stops, trading after a loss, or entering without a setup.
  • It builds discipline. Knowing you must write down every trade makes you think before entering.
  • It tells you whether your strategy works after enough trades.

"R" means your risk on the trade. If you risked $10 and made $20, the result is +2R. Measuring in R makes trades comparable even when lot sizes change. You can learn how to set the risk in our guide to calculating lot size.

How to review your journal

Weekly review (15 to 20 minutes)

  1. Count trades, wins and losses.
  2. Add up your total result in R.
  3. Mark every trade where you broke a rule and note why.
  4. Pick one thing to improve next week. Only one.

After 30 to 50 trades

Now the numbers become meaningful:

  • Win rate: wins divided by total trades.
  • Average win and average loss in R.
  • Expectancy: (win rate × average win in R) - (loss rate × average loss in R). A positive number means an edge. See risk reward ratio explained for examples.
  • Maximum drawdown: your biggest fall from a peak. Read forex drawdown recovery explained to see why it matters.
  • Results by setup, pair and time of day. You may find that one setup makes all your money and another loses it.

Common journal mistakes

  • Only recording winners. Losers teach more.
  • Writing too much, then quitting after a week. Keep entries short.
  • Never reviewing. A journal you do not read is just storage.
  • Judging by one trade. Look at groups of 20 or more trades.
  • Skipping the emotion column, which often explains your worst trades.

Pair it with a checklist

A journal records what happened. A checklist prevents mistakes before they happen. Use both: go through the pre-trade checklist, then log the trade.

Frequently asked questions

What should a forex trading journal include?

Date, pair, direction, setup, entry, stop loss, take profit, lot size, risk percent, result in pips and R, whether you followed your plan, your emotions and a chart screenshot.

How many trades do I need before reviewing my journal?

Do a short review every week. For statistics such as win rate and expectancy, wait for at least 30 to 50 trades, and more is better.

Should I journal demo trades too?

Yes. Demo trades help you practice the habit and test your strategy. Remember that emotions feel different with real money.

Is a spreadsheet or a notebook better?

Either works. A spreadsheet makes calculations easier, a notebook is quick. Choose the one you will keep using.
