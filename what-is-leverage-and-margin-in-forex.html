What Is Leverage and Margin in Forex? A Beginner Guide

By Forex Profit Pathways · Updated 30 October 2026 · 8 min read
Short answer: Leverage lets you control a large position with a small amount of money. Margin is the part of your account the broker sets aside to keep that position open. With 1:100 leverage, $1,000 can control a $100,000 position, but your losses grow just as fast as your profits.

Leverage is the reason forex looks so attractive to beginners, and also the reason many of them lose their accounts. To trade safely you must understand both leverage and margin.

What is leverage?

Leverage is a loan-like facility from your broker that lets you open a trade larger than your own money. It is shown as a ratio such as 1:30, 1:100 or 1:500.

With 1:100 leverage, every $1 of your money can control $100 in the market. So $1,000 can control a position worth $100,000.

What is margin?

Margin is not a fee. It is a deposit, taken from your account balance and locked while your trade is open. When you close the trade, the margin is released.

Required margin = position value / leverage

Margin example

You buy 1 standard lot (100,000 units) of EUR/USD at 1.0850 on a USD account.

  • Position value: 100,000 × 1.0850 = $108,500
  • With 1:100 leverage: $108,500 / 100 = $1,085 margin
  • With 1:30 leverage: $108,500 / 30 = about $3,617 margin

The same trade needs more margin when leverage is lower.

Leverage magnifies profits and losses

Leverage does not change how much a pip is worth. Your lot size does that (see what is lot size in forex). What leverage changes is how large a position you are allowed to open. The danger is that it makes oversized positions easy.

Example: with a $1,000 account, a 1 lot position on EUR/USD is worth about $10 per pip. A move of only 100 pips against you is a $1,000 loss, which is the whole account. Leverage allowed you to open it, but it did not protect you.

Key margin terms

TermMeaning
BalanceYour money without open trades
EquityBalance plus or minus the floating profit or loss
Used marginMoney locked for open trades
Free marginEquity minus used margin, available for new trades
Margin levelEquity / used margin × 100

Margin call and stop out

If your trades lose and your margin level falls too low, the broker warns you with a margin call. If it keeps falling to the broker's stop out level, the platform starts closing your trades automatically, usually the losing ones first. The exact levels differ by broker, so check yours before you trade.

How much leverage should you use?

The leverage your broker offers is a maximum, not a target. What matters is your effective leverage: your total position value divided by your account equity. A $1,000 account with a $5,000 position is only 5:1 effective leverage, even if the broker offers 1:500. Many careful traders keep effective leverage low and limit risk to about 1% per trade.

Rules also differ by country. For example, retail leverage on major pairs is capped at 1:30 in the EU and UK, and at 1:50 in the United States. Check the rules that apply where you live and where your broker is regulated.

How to use leverage safely

  1. Decide risk per trade first (for example 1% of the account).
  2. Set the stop loss from the chart and calculate the lot size with the Lot Size Calculator.
  3. Check that the required margin leaves plenty of free margin.
  4. Avoid opening many trades in the same direction on related pairs.
  5. Plan the trade with the Risk Reward Calculator before you enter.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Choosing a broker for the highest leverage number.
  • Using nearly all free margin on one trade.
  • Believing leverage is free money. It increases risk, not skill.
  • Not knowing the broker's margin call and stop out levels.

Frequently asked questions

What does 1:100 leverage mean?

It means you can control a position 100 times larger than the margin you put up. $1,000 of margin can control a $100,000 position.

Is margin a cost I pay to the broker?

No. Margin is a deposit that is locked while the trade is open and released when you close it. Your costs are the spread, commission and any swap fees.

What is a margin call?

A margin call is a warning from the broker that your account equity has fallen too low compared with your used margin. If it keeps falling, your trades can be closed automatically.

Is higher leverage better?

Not necessarily. Higher leverage lets you open bigger positions, which also means bigger losses. Your lot size and stop loss decide your real risk.
