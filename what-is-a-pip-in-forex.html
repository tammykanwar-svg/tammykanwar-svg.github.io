What Is a Pip in Forex? A Simple Guide with Examples

By Forex Profit Pathways · Updated 30 October 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: A pip is the standard unit used to measure price movement in forex. For most currency pairs, 1 pip equals 0.0001. For pairs that include the Japanese yen, 1 pip equals 0.01.

If you have just started learning forex, the word "pip" shows up everywhere: in charts, in broker platforms, in stop loss and take profit settings. Once you understand pips, you can measure your trades, size your positions, and control your risk much more clearly.

What does pip mean?

Pip stands for "percentage in point" (some sources say "price interest point"). It is the smallest standard step in which a currency pair's price is quoted. Instead of saying "the price moved from 1.0850 to 1.0880," traders simply say "the price moved 30 pips."

How to count pips

Look at the price and find the right decimal place.

Most pairs (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD)

The pip is the fourth decimal place. If EUR/USD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0880, that is a move of 30 pips.

Yen pairs (USD/JPY, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY)

The pip is the second decimal place. If USD/JPY moves from 150.20 to 150.70, that is a move of 50 pips.

What is a pipette?

Many brokers show one extra decimal place (the fifth on most pairs, the third on yen pairs). That fractional digit is called a pipette, and it equals one tenth of a pip. So a price of 1.08505 has a pipette digit of 5.

How much is a pip worth?

A pip is a distance, not an amount of money. How much money one pip is worth is called the pip value, and it depends on your position size (lot size) and the pair you trade.

The basic pip value formula

When the quote currency (the second currency in the pair) is the same as your account currency, the formula is simple:

Pip value = pip size × position size in units

Example on EUR/USD with a USD account and a mini lot: 0.0001 × 10,000 = $1 per pip.

When the quote currency is different

For pairs like USD/JPY the result first comes out in yen, so you convert it to your account currency. For one standard lot of USD/JPY: 0.01 × 100,000 = 1,000 yen per pip. If USD/JPY is trading at 150, then 1,000 / 150 is roughly $6.67 per pip. Pip value changes a little as the exchange rate moves.

A full trade example

Say you buy EUR/USD at 1.0850 using a mini lot ($1 per pip). You set your take profit at 1.0900 and your stop loss at 1.0820.

  • Take profit distance: 50 pips, so a potential gain of about $50
  • Stop loss distance: 30 pips, so a potential loss of about $30
  • Reward to risk: 50 / 30, roughly 1.67 to 1

This is why pips matter. They let you compare the size of your potential profit and loss before you enter. You can test your own numbers with the Risk Reward Calculator.

Why pips matter for risk management

Professional traders usually decide how much money they are willing to risk first (for example 1% of the account), then work backwards:

  1. Decide the stop loss distance in pips, based on the chart.
  2. Decide the dollar amount you are willing to lose.
  3. Calculate the lot size so that the stop loss distance equals that dollar amount.

Common beginner mistakes with pips

  • Mixing up pips and profit. 50 pips can be $5 or $500 depending on lot size.
  • Forgetting yen pairs are different. A 0.50 move on USD/JPY is 50 pips, not 5.
  • Ignoring the spread. The spread is the cost of the trade, measured in pips. A 2 pip spread means you start the trade 2 pips behind.
  • Setting stops by feeling. A random 10 pip stop on a volatile pair is often hit by normal noise. Base it on the chart.

Frequently asked questions

How much is 1 pip worth?

On EUR/USD, about $10 for a standard lot, $1 for a mini lot, and $0.10 for a micro lot. Other pairs differ slightly.

Is a pip the same as a point?

No. A pip is the standard unit. A point or pipette is a fractional pip, equal to one tenth of a pip on most brokers.

Why do yen pairs use a different pip size?

Yen pairs are quoted with two decimal places instead of four, so one pip is 0.01.

How many pips can I make in a day?

There is no fixed number. It depends on the pair, volatility, and your strategy, and many days a trader makes none or loses pips. Do not plan around a pip target; plan around risk per trade.

Key takeaways

  • A pip is 0.0001 for most pairs and 0.01 for yen pairs.
  • Pip value depends on lot size and the pair you trade.
  • Always measure your stop loss and take profit in pips, then convert to money before you trade.
