How to Calculate Lot Size in Forex: Step by Step with Examples

By Forex Profit Pathways · Updated 30 October 2026 · 8 min read
Short answer: Lot size = amount you risk / (stop loss in pips × pip value per lot). On a $1,000 account risking 1% ($10) with a 20 pip stop on EUR/USD, the lot size is 0.05.

Calculating your lot size before each trade is the most practical risk management habit in forex. It makes sure a losing trade costs only a planned amount. If you are not sure what lots are, read what lot size is in forex first.

The 4 inputs you need

  1. Account balance: for example $1,000.
  2. Risk per trade: a percent of your balance. Many traders use 1%.
  3. Stop loss in pips: from the chart, not from your wish.
  4. Pip value per standard lot: $10 on EUR/USD. For other pairs see how to calculate pip value.

The formula

Risk amount = balance × risk %

Lot size = risk amount / (stop loss pips × pip value per lot)

Example 1: EUR/USD

Balance $5,000, risk 1%, stop loss 25 pips.

  • Risk amount: $5,000 × 1% = $50
  • Lot size: $50 / (25 × $10) = 50 / 250 = 0.20 lots

Example 2: USD/JPY

Balance $1,000, risk 1%, stop loss 30 pips. At a rate of 150, one standard lot of USD/JPY is worth about $6.67 per pip.

  • Risk amount: $10
  • Lot size: $10 / (30 × $6.67) = 10 / 200 = 0.05 lots

Example 3: small account and rounding

Balance $500, risk 1%, stop loss 20 pips on EUR/USD.

  • Risk amount: $5
  • Lot size: 5 / (20 × 10) = 0.025 lots

Most brokers allow steps of 0.01, so you round down to 0.02 lots. Rounding up would risk more than you planned. If the minimum lot at your broker is larger than the result, the trade is too big for your plan and you should skip it or widen your account size.

The same thing using units

For pairs where USD is the quote currency you can also work in units:

Units = risk amount / (stop loss pips × 0.0001)

For $10 risk and a 20 pip stop on EUR/USD: 10 / (20 × 0.0001) = 5,000 units, which is 0.05 lots.

Step by step checklist

  1. Find your entry and stop loss on the chart.
  2. Measure the stop loss in pips.
  3. Decide your risk percent and risk amount.
  4. Calculate the lot size and round down.
  5. Check the reward. See our risk reward ratio guide.
  6. Check the margin needed. See leverage and margin in forex.

Common mistakes

  • Using one fixed lot for every trade, even when the stop loss changes.
  • Setting a very tight stop only to get a bigger lot size.
  • Forgetting that the spread adds to your real risk, especially on short stops.
  • Not recalculating when the pair changes, because pip value differs by pair.

Frequently asked questions

What is the lot size formula in forex?

Lot size = risk amount divided by (stop loss in pips multiplied by pip value per lot). The risk amount is your balance multiplied by your risk percent.

What lot size should I use for a $100 account?

It depends on your stop loss. With 1% risk that is only $1, so you need a very small stop or a nano or cent account. Many small accounts cannot follow strict risk rules with a normal stop loss.

Should I round the lot size up or down?

Down. Rounding up risks more than the amount you planned.

How much of my account should I risk per trade?

Many traders risk 1% or less. The right level depends on your strategy and experience.
