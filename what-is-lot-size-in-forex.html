What Is Lot Size in Forex? Standard, Mini and Micro Lots Explained

By Forex Profit Pathways · Updated 30 October 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer: A lot is the size of a forex trade. One standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency, a mini lot is 10,000 units, and a micro lot is 1,000 units. The bigger the lot, the more each pip is worth, and the faster your account can grow or shrink.

Lot size is one of the first things a new trader must understand, because it decides how much money you win or lose on every pip of movement. Many beginners lose their accounts not because their analysis is bad, but because their lot size is too big for their balance.

What is a lot in forex?

Forex is traded in fixed packages called lots. When you buy 1 lot of EUR/USD, you are buying 100,000 euros against US dollars. The lot is simply the unit that measures your position size.

In most trading platforms such as MetaTrader, you type the lot size as a decimal. So 0.01 is a micro lot and 0.10 is a mini lot. If you are new to pips, read our guide on what a pip is in forex first.

How lot size affects your profit and loss

Imagine EUR/USD moves 30 pips against you.

  • With a micro lot (0.01), the loss is about $3.
  • With a mini lot (0.10), the loss is about $30.
  • With a standard lot (1.00), the loss is about $300.

The market did exactly the same thing in all three cases. Only the lot size changed the result.

How to choose the right lot size

Do not choose a lot size because it feels right. Choose it from your risk. A common rule is to risk only 1% of your account on a single trade. Professionals often risk even less.

Step by step

  1. Decide how much you can risk. On a $1,000 account, 1% is $10.
  2. Decide your stop loss distance in pips from the chart. Say 20 pips.
  3. Divide: risk amount / (stop loss pips × pip value per standard lot).

Lots = $10 / (20 pips × $10) = 0.05 lots

Check: 0.05 lots is 5,000 units, worth about $0.50 per pip. A 20 pip stop loss costs 20 × $0.50 = $10, which is exactly 1% of the account.

Lot size and margin

Your lot size also decides how much margin your broker locks while the trade is open. Bigger lots need more margin. We explain this in our guide to leverage and margin in forex.

Common lot size mistakes

  • Using the same lot for every trade. A 50 pip stop and a 10 pip stop need different lot sizes to risk the same money.
  • Choosing the lot first and the stop second. Always set the stop from the chart, then calculate the lot.
  • Trading standard lots on a small account. A $500 account with 1 lot can be wiped out by a normal market move.
  • Increasing the lot after a loss to win it back. This is how small losses become large ones.

Which lot size should a beginner start with?

Most beginners do best with micro lots (0.01) on a demo account first, then a small live account. The goal at the start is to learn your process, not to make big money. Our risk reward ratio guide shows how to plan each trade before you enter.

Frequently asked questions

How many units are in 1 lot?

One standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency. A mini lot is 10,000 units and a micro lot is 1,000 units.

What is 0.01 lot in forex?

0.01 lot is a micro lot, which is 1,000 units. On EUR/USD each pip is worth about $0.10.

What lot size should I use for a $100 account?

Most traders use 0.01 lot or smaller on very small accounts, and size it so the stop loss risks only about 1% of the balance. Even then, small accounts leave little room for error.

Does lot size change by currency pair?

The lot is always 100,000 units of the base currency, but the pip value changes with the pair, so the same lot can be worth a different amount per pip.
